Dawn Aerowar is an interactive animated film — a project that blends 3D animation, hand-drawn 2D art, and full voice acting into a nearly two-hour story told across 12 episodes. It launches on Steam for Windows on November 10, 2026, and it plays more like a movie you watch than a game you master. If you've been wondering whether it's a film or a game, how long it runs, or what the story is about, this guide covers everything confirmed so far from the official site.

## What Dawn Aerowar Is About

The story follows Zodiac 1, a fighter pilot on his final mission, driven by revenge against the Saal'Runa Empire — the force that took his family, his home, and his purpose. His job: escort a weapon that no one should know exists across the whole Empire, from the northern front to the strait, the Aegis Sea, and the Capital.

Standing in his way are three aces, the last pilots still flying after years of war. Each of them asks Zodiac the same haunting question: do you even know what you're fighting for?

The world itself is grim. The Free Nations stand on the brink of annihilation, and the Empire's iron fist has crushed nearly all resistance. All but one.

## Film or Game? How the Interactive Film Actually Works

Dawn is a film first, and the official site is upfront about it: you sit back, turn the volume up, and watch the story unfold. But "interactive" isn't just marketing — here's how the experience is structured:

- **Player mode:** Choose each of the 12 episodes from a map, giving you control over how you experience the story.
- **Cinematic mode (War Room):** Plays the entire film from start to finish with no radio interruptions and no returns to the map — a pure movie viewing.
- **Controls:** Keyboard, mouse, or gamepad.

In other words, it's a watchable film with player-driven structure layered on top, rather than a game with cutscenes bolted on.

## Animation Style: 3D Dogfights and Hand-Drawn Cockpits

The film's visual identity comes from mixing two distinct styles:

- **3D animation** stages and shoots every aerial sequence like a real film, capturing the war in the air from the northern front to the Capital.
- **2D hand-drawn art** handles the faces and cockpits — the intimate, human moments inside the aircraft.

This combination lets the film swing between sweeping air battles and quiet character work without ever breaking its cinematic tone.

## Voices, Radio Drama, and the AWACS Operator

Zodiac flies alone, and the war reaches him through one channel: the cockpit radio. An AWACS operator he's never met — sharp, talkative, and strangely well informed — becomes his only connection to the conflict. Through that single voice, Zodiac (and the audience) hears the whole war.

Beyond that central performance, a professional voice cast brings hardened pilots and ruthless commanders to life, with every line fully performed and more than thirty additional voices in the mix. Voice acting is in English, with subtitles available in English, French, Italian, German, and Spanish.

## The Soundtrack: 77 Minutes of Original Music

The score, composed by Jose Pavli, was written to picture — a fast-paced, epic mix featuring choirs, Bulgarian voices, and classical guitar, plus two original songs. It runs 77 minutes. If the aerial footage is the body of the film, this soundtrack is clearly meant to be its pulse.
