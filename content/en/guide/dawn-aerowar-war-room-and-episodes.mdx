Dawn Aerowar isn't a traditional game — it's an interactive animated film told across 12 episodes over nearly two hours, and how you experience those episodes is the heart of its design. According to the official site, you can pick episodes individually from a map in the player, or sit back and let the whole story play out in one continuous sitting. The War Room represents that interactive side of the experience, while Cinematic mode handles the pure movie watch-through.

Here's a full breakdown of what's confirmed about the episode structure, the two viewing modes, and what to expect when the film launches on Steam for Windows on November 10, 2026.

## What Dawn Aerowar Actually Is

Dawn Aerowar blends 3D animation, hand-drawn 2D art, and full voice acting to tell the story of a fighter pilot on his final mission. The pilot, callsign Zodiac 1, escorts a weapon no one should know exists while the aces of the Saal'Runa Empire — the last pilots standing after years of war — try to stop him.

The framing is deliberately passive: you sit back, turn the volume up, and watch the story unfold. The interactivity comes from how you choose to move through the episodes, not from piloting the aircraft yourself.

## The 12-Episode Structure

The film is divided into 12 episodes covering a single last sortie across the entire Empire. Zodiac's flight travels from the northern front to the strait, the Aegis Sea, and finally the Capital. Each flight is staged and animated in 3D and shot like a film.

Key confirmed details about the format:

- **Runtime:** Nearly two hours total
- **Episodes:** 12
- **Languages:** English voice acting, with subtitles in English, French, Italian, German, and Spanish
- **Platforms:** Steam, Windows
- **Controls:** Keyboard, mouse, or gamepad

## The War Room and Episode Selection

The player's map-based interface is where the interactive element lives. Rather than forcing a strict linear watch, you can choose each episode from a map in the player. This gives you control over pacing — whether you binge the whole sortie in one sitting or experience it in smaller chunks.

The official site's War Room section is brief, but it clarifies the key distinction: Cinematic mode plays the film from start to finish, with no radio and no return to the map. In other words, Cinematic mode strips away the interactive layer entirely for an uninterrupted, film-like presentation. The radio chatter — delivered by an AWACS operator Zodiac has never met, who is sharp, talkative, and strangely well informed — is part of how the story reaches the pilot, and Cinematic mode removes it.

## Story: Revenge, Three Aces, and One Question

Zodiac 1 lost his family, his home, and his purpose to the Empire. Revenge is the only thing keeping him in the cockpit. Three aces have been sent to stop him, and each asks him the same question: do you even know what you're fighting for?

That question drives the emotional core of the story, and the hand-drawn 2D art handles the faces and cockpit scenes while 3D animation covers the aerial combat.

## Soundtrack and Voice Cast

The score is 77 minutes of original music composed to picture by Jose Pavli — fast-paced and epic, featuring choirs, Bulgarian voices, classical guitar, and two original songs. A professional voice cast performs every line, with over thirty additional voices beyond the main characters.

The project was 186% funded on Kickstarter by more than 600 backers, and Pavli also produced, directed, and edited the film. The script was written by Matthew "FlyAwayNow" Nguyen.

## Release Date and Availability

Dawn Aerowar launches on Steam for Windows on **November 10, 2026**. You can find more details and wishlist the film on the [official Dawn Aerowar website](https://dawnaerowar.com/). Keyboard, mouse, and gamepad are all supported, so you can control the player however you prefer.
