The wait is nearly over for fans of aerial-combat storytelling: **DAWN AEROWAR's release date is confirmed for November 10, 2026 on Steam**. That's just days away as of this writing, making it one of the most intriguing launches of the month for anyone who loves flight-combat narratives.

What makes this release unusual is the format. DAWN AEROWAR isn't a flight sim or an arcade shooter — it's an interactive animated film, blending 3D animation, hand-drawn 2D art, and full voice acting into a single, structured story about one pilot's final mission.

## What Is DAWN AEROWAR?

DAWN AEROWAR tells the story of Zodiac 1, a fighter pilot on a revenge mission against the Saal'Runa Empire — the force that took his family, his home, and his purpose. His final sortie carries him across the entire Empire, from the northern front through the strait, the Aegis Sea, and the Capital, escorting a weapon no one should know exists.

The film runs nearly two hours across 12 episodes. Every flight sequence is staged and animated in 3D and shot like a movie, while faces, cockpits, and cockpit close-ups are hand drawn in 2D. If you've ever wanted a war-in-the-sky drama you could actually sit down and watch, this is it — with just enough interactivity to keep you in the pilot's seat.

## Meet the Aces Hunting You

Standing between Zodiac and his goal are three Imperial aces, and each one frames the film's central question: does he even know what he's fighting for?

- **Reaper**, the Name of Death — loves war more than peace
- **Freya**, the Imperial Goddess — quotes philosophers mid-dogfight
- **Miracle**, the Guardian Angel — known for the lives she saved, not the ones she took

Backing the drama is a fully voiced cast of professionals, including Sam Hughes, Kira Buckland, Lara Sawalha, Blythe Melin, Sandra Espinoza, Mark Mullens, and Jeremiah Foltz, plus more than thirty additional voices. Zodiac's only companion in the air is a talkative, sharp, and strangely well-informed AWACS operator on the radio — and their conversations carry much of the war's emotional weight.

## A Soundtrack by the Project Wingman Composer

Music fans have an extra reason to pay attention. The original 77-minute score is composed to picture by **Jose Pavli, the composer of Project Wingman**. It's orchestral, fast-paced, and epic — featuring choirs, Bulgarian voices, classical guitar, and two original songs.

## How the "Interactive" Part Works

Between episodes, you land back on a map where the route to your next sortie draws itself and a debrief tallies what you collected. The interactivity is light but rewarding:

- **12 intelligence dossiers** to unlock, with images and service records
- **458 radio transmissions** archived in a Communications log, playable back through an in-game receiver
- **Achievements** tied to what you intercept, open, and hear during the film

Prefer a pure movie experience? Cinematic mode — available in the War Room, the player, or the settings — plays the entire film start to finish with no radio and no map interludes.
